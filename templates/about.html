About the Research

Modelling UK Government Debt, Bond Markets and Wealth Transfer using Agent-Based Simulation

Introduction

Government debt represents the total amount of money borrowed by the UK Government to finance public expenditure whenever public spending exceeds tax revenue. Governments borrow by issuing Government Bonds (Gilts), which are purchased by banks, institutional investors, pension funds and households.

Increasing government debt influences taxation, public expenditure, inflation, interest rates and long-term economic growth. Understanding these relationships is essential for evaluating fiscal sustainability and economic policy.

UK Government Bond Market

Government Bonds are debt securities issued by the UK Government to finance borrowing requirements. Investors purchasing these bonds receive regular interest payments until maturity.

Bond yields respond to changes in inflation, monetary policy, investor confidence and market demand. Rising yields increase the government's borrowing costs, whereas lower yields reduce financing costs.

Wealth Transfer

Government borrowing redistributes wealth throughout the economy. Interest payments are transferred to investors holding government bonds, while taxation and government expenditure affect household income and overall economic welfare.

The interaction between Government, Banks, Investors and Households determines how wealth circulates through the financial system.

Agent-Based Modelling

Agent-Based Modelling (ABM) is a computational modelling technique in which individual economic agents interact according to behavioural rules. Instead of analysing the economy as one system, ABM models decisions made by individual participants.

This research models four economic agents:

  • Government
  • Banks
  • Investors
  • Households

Their interactions determine Government Debt, Bond Yield and Wealth Distribution over time.

Monte Carlo Simulation

Monte Carlo Simulation repeatedly executes the Agent-Based Model under randomly changing economic conditions. This allows uncertainty analysis and evaluates the robustness of simulation results.

The distribution of Government Debt, Bond Yield and Wealth outcomes helps identify possible future economic scenarios.

Network Analysis

Network Analysis visualises relationships among Government, Banks, Investors and Households. These economic connections illustrate the movement of debt, bond investments, interest payments and wealth transfer throughout the economy.

Research Objectives
  1. Analyse historical UK Government Debt.
  2. Model Government Bond Market behaviour.
  3. Simulate interactions between Government, Banks, Investors and Households.
  4. Investigate Wealth Transfer within the UK economy.
  5. Evaluate uncertainty using Monte Carlo Simulation.
  6. Visualise economic relationships using Network Analysis.
